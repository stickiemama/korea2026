The cards

Snack stories

Egg bread

Gyeran-ppang

A small, sweet, fluffy bun baked with a whole egg on top, sometimes with cheese or ham. It's a classic cold-weather snack, best eaten piping hot.

Weird but true

Bread disguised as a potato

Gamja-ppang is made to look exactly like a small potato, dusted with cocoa or grain powder for the "skin." Inside it's chewy and often filled with cheese or mashed potato. It's a famous snack from Gangwon province, Korea's potato-growing region.

Winter favourite

Fish without fish

Did you spot the fish-shaped pastries? That's bungeoppang, "carp bread," filled with sweet red bean or custard, but no fish. It came to Korea from a similar Japanese snack in the 20th century. Koreans love debating whether to eat the head or the tail first.

Street culture

Pojangmacha

Street stalls under orange tents are called pojangmacha, "covered wagons." After the Korean War, cheap food from carts and tents fed a hungry, busy city, and the tradition lives on in markets and shopping streets.

Fusion

Old snacks, new flavours

Seoul stalls constantly invent new twists, like Nutella-filled crispy pastries or cheese-stuffed breads. Trends spread fast through social media, so the snack of the season can change from one year to the next.
