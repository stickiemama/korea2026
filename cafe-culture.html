Part 1

How coffee came to Korea

History

The king who loved coffee

Coffee arrived in Korea in the late 1800s. King Gojong is said to have grown fond of it while sheltering at the Russian legation in Seoul in 1896. Later he had a Western-style pavilion, Jeonggwanheon, built at Deoksugung Palace, where he is said to have enjoyed coffee with guests.

On our trip: Deoksugung is on the "if there’s time" list in Seoul.
History

Dabang: the old tea rooms

From the 1920s to the 1970s, Koreans met in dabang, tea and coffee rooms where writers, artists and students gathered. Later dabang often had a DJ booth playing records, and they served sweet, milky coffee.

Invention

The coffee-mix stick

In 1976, a Korean company created the "coffee mix": instant coffee, creamer and sugar in one paper stick. Just add hot water. It's still in nearly every Korean office, home and hiking-trail rest stop.

Look for: yellow coffee-mix sticks in convenience stores and at your accommodation.
Part 2

Why cafés are everywhere

Everyday life

The living room you rent by the hour

Many Koreans live in small apartments with family, so cafés became the place to meet friends, go on dates, work or study. Students even have a word for studying in cafés: kagong, short for "café study."

Slang

"Iced Americano, even if I freeze"

Koreans drink iced Americanos all year round, even in winter. There's a slang word for it, eoljuka, short for "even if I freeze to death, iced Americano."

Count: how many people carry iced drinks on a cold day.
Jeju

View cafés

Jeju is famous for "view cafés": cafés with huge windows facing the sea, oreums or tangerine orchards, often in converted stone houses, warehouses or old hotels. People travel to Jeju partly to hop between them.

Our visit

Seogwipo Bakery, in the typhoon

On Sun 25 Oct we sheltered from Typhoon Champi at Seogwipo Bakery, a lovely café by the beach. Watching the storm waves through the window was the view-café experience, Jeju style.

Journal: what did everyone order, and what was the best thing on the menu?
Jeju menu

Only-in-Jeju drinks

Look for drinks made from island ingredients: hallabong ade (fizzy citrus), Jeju green tea lattes from the tea fields near Osulloc, and peanut lattes or ice cream made with peanuts from Udo, the cow-shaped island near Seongsan.

Viral

Dalgona coffee

In 2020, whipped "dalgona coffee" went viral around the world. It's named after dalgona, the crunchy honeycomb candy that later appeared in Squid Game, because the foam looks and tastes a bit like it.

How it works

Café manners

Order and pay at the counter, then wait for your buzzer to flash. Many cafés serve dine-in drinks in mugs or glasses and keep paper cups for takeaway. People happily leave laptops and phones on tables to save seats, because theft is rare.
