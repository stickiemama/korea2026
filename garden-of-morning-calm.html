The cards

About the garden

Name

Land of the Morning Calm

"Land of the Morning Calm" is an old nickname for Korea, from the name Joseon, often read as "fresh morning." It became famous in English through an 1885 book by the American traveller Percival Lowell, Chosön: The Land of the Morning Calm.

The founder

A professor’s dream

The garden was created by Han Sang-kyung, a horticulture professor, together with his wife, and opened in 1996. He wanted a garden that showed off Korean beauty: gentle curves, natural shapes and plants that suit the seasons, rather than straight lines and perfect lawns.

Look for

A garden shaped like Korea

One part of the garden is planted in the shape of the Korean peninsula, best seen from a viewpoint above it. Can you find Jeju, and where Seoul would be?

Seasons

A garden for every season

Spring brings azaleas and tulips, summer is lush with hostas and lotus, autumn turns the maples red and gold, and in winter the whole garden glows with millions of lights at the Lighting Festival.

Weird but true

Why maples turn red

Leaves are always full of yellow and orange pigments, hidden by green chlorophyll. In autumn the tree stops making chlorophyll, so the yellows show through. Maples also make new red pigments, which act like sunscreen while the tree pulls nutrients back from its leaves.
