The cards

Before you go

Flower

A flower that blooms in winter

Camellias, dongbaek in Korean, flower in the cold months, from late autumn into early spring, when almost nothing else blooms. In late October you may spot the first early varieties opening. Their glossy, waxy leaves stay green all year.

Surprise

Tea is a camellia!

The tea plant at Osulloc is Camellia sinensis, a close cousin of these garden camellias. Green tea, black tea and oolong all come from its leaves. Compare the leaves here with the tea bushes at Osulloc.

Autumn

Pink clouds of grass

The fluffy pink haze is pink muhly grass, originally from North America. In autumn its tiny flower heads turn pink and purple, and with the sun behind it the whole field seems to glow.

Photo tip: stand so the light comes through the grass from behind.
Remembering

A flower that falls whole

Most flowers drop their petals one by one, but a camellia falls as a whole flower, still bright red. In Jeju, the red camellia has become the symbol for remembering the people who died during the 4.3 events of 1948–49. You may see camellia badges and pictures around the island.

Old Korea

Camellia oil

In the past, Korean women pressed camellia seeds for oil to smooth and shine their hair. Camellia oil is still used in Korean skincare today, so check the Olive Young haul.

Mission

Garden detectives

  1. Find a camellia flower already open. What colour is it?
  2. Feel a camellia leaf. Is it waxy? Compare it with a tea leaf at Osulloc.
  3. Take a glowing pink-grass photo with the sun behind it.
  4. Find a fallen camellia flower lying whole on the ground.
