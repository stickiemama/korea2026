Our hike

Our hike

Up to Manse Hill and back

On Wed 28 Oct we climbed from Eorimok through the forest and out onto the open highlands as far as Manse Hill, then came back down the same way: 7.24 km, 620 m of climbing, 3 hours 35 minutes. A steep climb, and the long wooden steps down were hard on the knees!

Fuel: onigiri on the way up. Korea’s version is samgak gimbap, the triangle rice balls in every convenience store.
Part 2

The cards

Name

The mountain that pulls down the Milky Way

Hallasan is usually explained as "a mountain so high it can pull down the Milky Way." At 1,947 m it's the highest point in South Korea.

Volcano

The whole island is the volcano

Hallasan is a shield volcano: runny lava spread out in all directions over a very long time and built the gentle cone that is Jeju itself. The oreums we've climbed are smaller vents on its sides, and the lava tube was one of its underground rivers.

Legend

The white deer lake

At the very top is a crater lake called Baengnokdam, "white deer lake." Legend says heavenly beings came down riding white deer to drink there. It's the same baengnok as the butcher's restaurant where we had dinner on Day 4.

Climate zones

Coast to cold in one climb

Climbing Hallasan is like travelling north. Near the coast it's warm enough for subtropical plants, then come broadleaf forests, then conifers, and finally windswept alpine shrubs near the top. That's why we walked out of the trees onto a bare, windy plateau.

Endangered

Korea's own fir tree

The Korean fir grows naturally only in Korea, and Hallasan has one of its biggest forests. Many of the trees on the upper slopes are dying, and scientists link it to warmer winters and stronger typhoons. Look for grey, bare trunks among the green.

Weird but true

The bamboo takeover

The knee-high green bamboo carpeting the highlands is Jeju dwarf bamboo. Since grazing horses and cattle were moved off the mountain, it has spread so fast that it is crowding out rarer alpine flowers.

Protected

A triple-crowned mountain

Hallasan is a UNESCO Biosphere Reserve, part of the Jeju Volcanic Island and Lava Tubes World Heritage site, and part of the Jeju Global Geopark, a rare triple recognition.
