The cards

About Namsan

Name

The south mountain

Namsan means "south mountain." When the Joseon kings chose Seoul as their capital in 1394, Namsan marked its southern edge, with the palaces to its north. The Korean national anthem even sings about the pine trees on Namsan standing firm through wind and frost.

Old-school texting

Beacon fires

At the top are the restored bongsudae, beacon-fire stations. In Joseon times, signal fires were passed from mountain to mountain all the way from the borders to Namsan, so news reached the king within about a day.

The number of fires was the message: one meant all was peaceful, two meant enemies were sighted, and five meant battle had begun.

The wall

Seoul City Wall

The old city wall, first built in 1396, runs over Namsan. It linked four mountains around the capital, Bugaksan, Naksan, Namsan and Inwangsan, in a loop of about 18.6 km. You can still walk long stretches of it.

Trip link: the Naksan stretch is near our Seoul stay.
The tower

From TV tower to landmark

N Seoul Tower was built in the early 1970s as a TV and radio broadcasting tower and opened to visitors in 1980. It still sends out broadcasts today, and at night it is lit up in different colours, sometimes to show the level of fine dust in the air.

Weird but true

A fence of padlocks

Thousands of "love locks" cover the railings near the tower, left by couples, friends and families who write their names on a padlock and lock it on. Some people throw away the key; please don't throw it off the mountain!
