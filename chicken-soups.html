Part 1

What we ate

Our lunch

A whole chicken in soup

If each chicken was small, stuffed with rice and served bubbling in a black stone pot, it was samgyetang, ginseng chicken soup. If a bigger chicken was boiled whole and shared, it was probably dakbaeksuk, often finished with rice cooked in the leftover broth into porridge.

Samgyetang

Ginseng chicken soup

A young chicken is stuffed with sticky rice, garlic, jujubes (red dates) and a root of ginseng, then simmered until the meat falls off the bones. You season it yourself at the table with salt and pepper.

Try: dipping the chicken in the little dish of salt and pepper.
Our lunch

Flat noodles in chicken soup

Flat noodles with shredded chicken and carrots in broth is dak-kalguksu. Kal means knife and guksu means noodles: the dough is rolled flat and cut by hand, which gives the noodles their soft, slightly uneven shape.

Our banchan

Bean sprouts, bean sauce, radish and kimchi

The bean sprouts were probably kongnamul muchim, sprouts tossed in sesame oil and garlic. The bean sauce was likely ssamjang or doenjang, made from fermented soybeans and good for dipping. The radish was probably kkakdugi, cubed radish kimchi, a classic partner for chicken soup.

Part 2

The stories behind the bowl

Weird but true

Hot soup for the hottest days

Koreans traditionally eat samgyetang on boknal, the three hottest days of summer (chobok, jungbok and malbok). The idea is iyeol chiyeol, "fight heat with heat": a steaming, nourishing soup helps you sweat and get your energy back. Queues outside samgyetang restaurants on those days can stretch down the street.

Ingredient

Korea's famous root

Korean ginseng has been prized for centuries as a health tonic, and was once so valuable it was sent as royal tribute and traded across Asia. It tastes slightly bitter and earthy. Look for ginseng drinks, candies and even ginseng chocolate in shops.

Everyday food

Noodles for rainy days

Many Koreans crave kalguksu on rainy days. It was a good match for the days after a typhoon. Some restaurants make the noodles in front of you, slapping and cutting the dough by hand.

Modern Korea

Chimaek: chicken and beer

Korea is also crazy about fried chicken. Chimaek (chicken + maekju, beer) is a favourite for watching football or hanging out by the Han River, where you can even have chicken delivered to your picnic spot. Korea reportedly has more fried chicken shops than the whole world has McDonald's.

On our trip: the Han River picnic on Wed 4 Nov. Chicken delivery, anyone?
