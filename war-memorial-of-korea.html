The cards

About the memorial

Outside

A bomber on the lawn

Around 100 big weapons stand outside: tanks like the Sherman and the Soviet T-34 that North Korea drove into Seoul in 1950, an F-86 Sabre and an F-4 Phantom, and a B-52 Stratofortress, one of only three B-52s on display outside the USA.

Measure it: walk under the B-52’s wing. It’s about 56 m from tip to tip.
The statue

The Statue of Brothers

In the plaza, two soldiers hug: an older brother in a South Korean uniform and a younger brother in a North Korean one. It was inspired by the true story of brothers who met on opposite sides of the battlefield, and it stands for a family, and a country, split in two.

Remember

The names on the walls

Long corridors carry the names of soldiers killed in the Korean War, including UN soldiers from around the world. Sixteen countries sent combat troops under the UN flag, and others sent medical teams. Look for the names of Australian, British, Canadian and New Zealand soldiers.

Long history

Not just one war

The museum covers thousands of years of battles, from ancient kingdoms to the Japanese invasions of the 1590s. That's where you'll find the full-size replica of Admiral Yi Sun-sin's turtle ship, the iron-roofed warship that helped defeat the Japanese navy.

Site

Built on an army base

The memorial opened in 1994 on the old headquarters of the Korean army in Yongsan. Next door is a former US Army base, now being turned into a huge public park.
