The cards

About the museum

Must see

The Room of Quiet Contemplation

A dark, quiet room holds just two 1,400-year-old gilt-bronze statues of a bodhisattva sitting deep in thought, one leg crossed and a finger touching the cheek. Both are National Treasures. The floor tilts very slightly and the walls are covered in earthy clay, so the room feels calm and hushed.

Try it: walk all the way round them slowly, and watch how their faces change.
Silla

Gold crowns of Silla

The Silla kingdom (57 BC–AD 935) was so rich in gold that foreigners called it a land of gold. Its kings and queens were buried with dazzling gold crowns, their branches shaped like trees and deer antlers and hung with jade and little gold spangles that shimmered when they moved.

Trip link: see the Korea, China & Japan page for the Three Kingdoms.
Goryeo

Jade-green celadon

Goryeo potters (918–1392) made celadon, pottery with a soft blue-green glaze the colour of jade. They invented an inlay technique, carving patterns like cranes and clouds and filling them with white and black clay. Even Chinese visitors, the masters of porcelain, admired its colour.

Weird but true

A pagoda indoors

A 13.5 m marble pagoda from 1348 stands inside the main hall. It was taken apart and smuggled to Japan in 1907, returned in 1918, and later moved indoors to protect it from Seoul's acid rain and pollution.

Design

A building like a fortress

The museum moved here in 2005. Its long building is inspired by Korean fortress walls, with a huge open-air stairway in the middle framing a view of Namsan and N Seoul Tower. Remember riding up there?
