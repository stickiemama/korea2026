The cards

Shell science

Science

Who makes a shell?

Shells are made by soft-bodied animals called molluscs, such as snails, clams and scallops. They build them from calcium carbonate (the same stuff as chalk), adding new layers at the edge as they grow, so the shell grows with the animal.

Weird but true

The sea in a shell?

The "sea" you hear in a shell isn't the ocean. The curved shell catches the sounds around you and makes some of them echo louder, which sounds like waves. Try it with a cupped hand or a mug: it works too!

Ancient

The nautilus

Nautiluses are relatives of octopuses that live inside a spiral shell divided into chambers. They pump gas in and out of the chambers to float up and down. Their family has existed for around 500 million years, older than the dinosaurs.

Maths

Nature's spirals

Many shells grow in a spiral that keeps the same shape as it gets bigger, a pattern also found in pinecones, sunflowers and galaxies. Look at the nautilus: each chamber is a bigger copy of the last.

Jeju link

Shells of the haenyeo

Remember the conch (turban snail) at Jungmun and the abalone in Jeju porridge? Those are molluscs too, and the shellfish the haenyeo dive for.
