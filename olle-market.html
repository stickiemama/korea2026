The cards

About the market

Name

A market every day

Maeil means "every day." Many traditional Korean markets are five-day markets (ojang), held only on days ending in certain numbers, like 2 and 7. This one is open daily. "Olle" was added to its name because the Jeju Olle walking trail passes close by.

Street food

Lobster with a blowtorch

Grilled lobster topped with cheese, then blasted with a blowtorch until it bubbles, is one of the market's most famous (and most photographed) snacks. Jeju is surrounded by sea, so seafood stalls are everywhere.

Snack

Egg buns

Gyeran-ppang, "egg bread," is a small, sweet, fluffy bun baked with a whole egg on top. It's a favourite winter street snack across Korea, best eaten hot.

Jeju

Black pork everything

Jeju's famous black pork turns up in buns, skewers and dumplings at the market, a quick way to try it without a full BBQ dinner. See Jeju on a Plate for the black pork story.

Tip

How to shop

Many stalls let you try a sample, and some sellers will give a little extra if you buy several. Have some cash handy, although most stalls also take cards.
