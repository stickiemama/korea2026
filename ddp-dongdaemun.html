The cards

About DDP and Dongdaemun

Architecture

A building with no corners

DDP (Dongdaemun Design Plaza) was designed by the Iraqi-British architect Zaha Hadid, famous for flowing, curving buildings. Its skin is made of around 45,000 aluminium panels, many of them slightly different shapes, so the whole building seems to ripple.

Try: find a straight line or a sharp corner on the outside. It’s harder than it sounds.
History underneath

A stadium, then a city wall

DDP stands where Dongdaemun Stadium used to be. The stadium had been built in the 1920s, during Japanese rule, on top of part of Seoul's old city wall. When builders dug it up, they uncovered the buried wall and an old water gate, and kept them on display beside DDP.

Look for: the stone wall and arched water gate in the park beside the building.
The gate

The Great East Gate

Dongdaemun means "Great East Gate." Its proper name is Heunginjimun, one of the main gates in the wall that circled Joseon-era Seoul, first built in 1398. The wall ran over Namsan and the hills around the city, and much of it can still be walked today.

Shopping

The market that never sleeps

Dongdaemun has been a market area for more than a century. Today it's packed with fashion malls and wholesale markets, and some stay open late into the night or until the early morning, when shop owners from all over Korea come to buy clothes in bulk.

Design

Seoul, design capital

Seoul was named a World Design Capital in 2010, and DDP was built as a home for design. It hosts exhibitions and Seoul Fashion Week, and its design museum shop is a good place for souvenirs, like Mum's bookmarks.
