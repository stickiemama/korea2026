Korea in 60 seconds

Origin story

Korea was founded by a bear. Sort of.

A bear and a tiger wanted to become human. The rule: stay in a dark cave for 100 days eating nothing but mugwort and garlic. The tiger quit. The bear stuck it out and became a woman, and her son Dangun founded the first Korean kingdom in 2333 BCE (according to legend).

Part 3

Don't be that tourist

Do

  • Give and take things with both hands, especially from older people.
  • Let elders start eating first.
  • Ask for more banchan (side dishes). Refills are free.
  • Say kamsahamnida (thank you). People notice.

Don't

  • Stick chopsticks upright in your rice. It looks like an offering at a funeral.
  • Tip. It isn't expected and can confuse people.
  • Panic when the lift has no 4th floor. 4 sounds like "death," so it's often labelled F.
  • Sit in the pink or priority seats on the subway.

Jeju 제주

A volcanic island that used to be its own country, the kingdom of Tamna, until Korea absorbed it in the 12th century.

Legend

Three guys climbed out of the ground

Jeju's founding myth says its first three people rose up out of holes in the earth. The holes are still there, at Samseonghyeol in Jeju City.

Jeju City · near Dongmun Market
Weird but true

No thieves, no beggars, no gates

Jeju has "three plenties" (wind, stones and women) and "three nothings" (thieves, beggars and gates). Instead of locking up, families laid wooden poles across the entrance. The number of poles told visitors how long they'd be away.

Look for: stone walls with three poles across the gap. That's a jeongnang.
History

Stone grandpas everywhere

Dol hareubang means "stone grandfather." These basalt guardians with bulging eyes and hands on their bellies protected villages. Try counting how many you spot this week.

Superpower

Grandmas who free-dive

The haenyeo dive for shellfish and octopus with no oxygen tanks, often in their 60s, 70s and 80s. When they surface they let out a high whistle called sumbisori. Their way of life is on UNESCO's heritage list.

Seongsan Ilchulbong · Sun 25
Nature

Seongsan Ilchulbong: born from the sea

About 5,000 years ago a volcano erupted underwater and built this crater. It was an island until sand filled the gap and joined it to Jeju.

Sun 25
Weird but true

Black pork had a gross past

Jeju pigs used to live under the outhouse and eat what fell through. The pigs eat normal food now, and the BBQ is famous.

Nature

Walking through a lava tunnel

Manjanggul formed when the outside of a lava river cooled and hardened while hot lava kept flowing inside. It's about 7.4 km long, and the lava column at the end is one of the biggest known.

Look for: "lava shelves" on the walls, which mark where the lava level used to be.
Mon 26
Nature

A forest older than Joseon

Bijarim has about 2,800 nutmeg yew trees, many 500 to 800 years old. Some were already old when King Sejong invented Hangul.

Mon 26
History

Tangerines fit for a king

In Joseon times, Jeju's tangerines were sent to the king as tribute. The hallabong is named after Hallasan because the bump on top looks like the mountain.

Tue 27
Legend

The mountain that lost its top

Hallasan is South Korea's highest mountain at 1,947 m. A hunter accidentally poked the sky god with his bow. The furious god ripped off the mountain's peak and threw it, and it landed as Sanbangsan. The hole it left is the crater lake Baengnokdam.

Wed 28 · see Sanbangsan on Thu 29
Legend

Bath time for heavenly nymphs

Cheonjeyeon means "Pond of the Emperor of Heaven." Seven nymphs were said to come down at night to bathe in the falls.

Look for: the seven nymphs carved on the Seonimgyo Bridge.
Wed 28
History

Shipwrecked for 13 years

In 1653 a Dutch trading ship wrecked near Yongmeori. The sailors weren't allowed to leave Korea. One of them, Hendrick Hamel, escaped 13 years later and wrote the first detailed European book about Korea.

Look for: the replica of his ship near the coast.
Thu 29
Legend

The quest for immortality

China's first emperor sent a man named Xu Fu to find the elixir of eternal life. Legend says he passed Jeongbang Falls and carved a message on the cliff. Seogwipo's name is said to mean the place where Xu "went back west."

Fri 30
Nature

Did someone build these?

No. The perfect hexagonal columns at Jusangjeolli formed when lava hit the sea and cracked into shapes as it cooled, like mud cracking in the sun.

Fri 30
Legend

A dragon frozen mid-escape

A dragon stole a magic jade bead from Hallasan. The mountain god shot it with an arrow as it flew to the sea, and it turned to stone. That's Yongduam, "Dragon Head Rock."

Seoul 서울

The capital since 1394: palaces, markets, mountains and 10 million people.

History

The palace that refused to stay down

Gyeongbokgung was built in 1395, burned in 1592 during a Japanese invasion, and left in ruins for about 270 years. It was rebuilt in 1867, then mostly torn down during Japanese rule, and it's still being restored today. Queen Min was assassinated here in 1895.

Look for: the statues of King Sejong and Admiral Yi Sun-sin, who beat Japanese fleets with armoured "turtle ships," outside on Gwanghwamun Square.
Mon 2
History

The rich neighbourhood

Bukchon means "north village." Joseon officials and nobles lived here, between the two main palaces. People still live in these houses, so keep your voices down.

Mon 2
Weird but true

They dug up a highway to free a stream

Cheonggyecheon was buried under a road and an elevated highway for decades. In 2005 Seoul tore down the highway and brought the stream back. Now people dip their feet in it.

Mon 2
History

Joseon's text message

Before phones, beacon fires on Namsan's summit passed signals across the kingdom: one fire meant all clear, more fires meant danger. A message could cross Korea in about half a day.

Wed 4
Weird but true

An island that calls itself a country

Nami Island only became an island in 1944, when a dam raised the river. It's named after General Nam I of the 1400s, and it calls itself the "Naminara Republic." A 2002 K-drama, Winter Sonata, made it famous across Asia.

Tue 3
History

A mountain fortress in the city

Bukhansan has a fortress wall from 1711, built so the king had somewhere to retreat to. It's one of the most visited national parks in the world.

Sun 1
Nature

The DMZ is a secret nature reserve

The strip between North and South is about 250 km long and 4 km wide. Almost no one has entered it for 70 years, so rare cranes and other wildlife thrive there. North Korea dug tunnels underneath; the third one was found in 1978, and you can walk inside it.

Thu 5 (if you go)
Food

Gwangjang Market, since 1905

One of Korea's oldest markets. Order bindaetteok (crispy mung bean pancakes) and mayak gimbap. "Mayak" means "drug," because people say they're addictive.

Thu 5
History

The spaceship at Dongdaemun

DDP was designed by the architect Zaha Hadid and opened in 2014 on the site of an old stadium. Excavations uncovered pieces of the Joseon city wall, which you can still see beside it.
