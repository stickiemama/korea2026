Part 1

The rail bike

How it works

A bike on train tracks

A rail bike is a little car that runs on rails, so there's no steering. Jeju's rail bikes used to be pedal-powered, but now they run on electric motors, so you just sit back and watch the view. The track loops through open countryside on what used to be farm and ranch land, so it's quiet, with few buildings or cars.

Plan: it may pause in heavy rain or strong wind, so check before driving over.
Our ride

Bumped from behind!

On our ride on Mon 26 Oct, the rail car behind us ran into the back of ours. A scary jolt, but we were all wearing our seatbelts and nobody was hurt. Lesson learned: always buckle up, even on a slow ride.

Spot them

Four famous views

From the track you can see Yongnuni Oreum, Udo (the cow-shaped island), Seongsan Ilchulbong and rows of wind turbines. How many can you spot in one photo?

Science

Why so many wind turbines?

East Jeju is one of the windiest places in Korea, which is why the stone walls have gaps and the roofs were tied down. Today that wind powers turbines on land and at sea. Jeju has set itself the goal of becoming a carbon-free island, powered by clean energy.

Remember: the turbines off Woljeongri Beach.
Part 2

Yongnuni Oreum

Name

"Dragon's eye" hill

Yongnuni means something like "dragon's eye." The hill has several craters side by side, and from above, people said it looked like a dragon lying down, or like the hollow of a dragon's eye.

Autumn

Silver grass season

In late October the oreum is covered in eoksae, silver grass, which glows gold and silver when the sun is low. That's why photographers love it at this time of year.

Try: a photo looking back at the grass with the light behind it.
Nature

One of Jeju's 360 oreums

An oreum is a small volcanic cone that grew on the side of the bigger Hallasan volcano. Jeju has around 360 of them. Yongnuni is gentle, with soft grassy slopes rather than rock. Some trails close from time to time to let the grass recover, so follow the signs.

Photo history

The photographer's hill

Photographer Kim Young-gap spent years photographing Jeju's oreums, and Yongnuni was one of his favourites. His gallery, Dumoak, in an old school in the east of Jeju, shows his wind-swept pictures of the island.
