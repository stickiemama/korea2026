Our dinner

Sirloin at Baengnok Butcher Restaurant

On Tue 27 Oct, near our second Jeju home, we grilled Korean sirloin (deungsim) at Baengnok Butcher Restaurant. The verdict: very close in quality to the Australian wagyu we enjoy at home.

Name spotting: Baengnok means "white deer." Hallasan’s crater lake, Baengnokdam, means "white deer lake," from a legend of heavenly beings who came down riding white deer to drink there.
How it works

The butcher's restaurant

At a jeongyuk-sikdang (butcher's restaurant) you often choose your meat at a butcher's counter and pay by weight, then pay a small table charge for the grill, side dishes and sauces. It's usually cheaper than a regular BBQ restaurant for the same quality.

The breed

What is Hanwoo?

Hanwoo is Korea's native breed of cattle, with reddish-brown coats, which has lived on the peninsula for over 2,000 years. It's Korea's most prized beef: well marbled, with a rich, beefy flavour, and expensive because there's little farmland to raise it.

History

From farm worker to feast

Until the 1960s, Korea was mainly a farming country, and cows were precious workers that pulled ploughs and carts. A family's cow was its savings account, sold only when money was badly needed. Eating beef was a luxury, which is why Hanwoo still means celebration and is a popular holiday gift.

Grading

What 1++ means

Korean beef is graded on marbling (the white flecks of fat), meat colour, fat colour, firmness and maturity. The top grades are 1++, 1+ and 1. The very best is 1++ with the highest marbling score, the top few percent of all cattle.

Look for: the grade on the label or menu at the butcher’s counter.
Compare

Hanwoo vs wagyu

Japanese wagyu is famous for extremely heavy marbling that melts in your mouth. Hanwoo usually has a little less fat and a stronger beef flavour. Australian wagyu comes from Japanese wagyu genetics, often crossed with other breeds, which is partly why it tasted so familiar.

Jeju

Jeju's black cattle

Jeju also has its own rare native black cattle, heugu, as black as the island's black pigs. There are so few that the breed is protected, and you may occasionally see heugu beef on Jeju menus at a high price.

How to eat

Scissors and lettuce wraps

Korean BBQ comes with scissors for cutting meat on the grill. Wrap a piece in lettuce or perilla leaf with garlic, a dab of ssamjang and a little rice, then eat it in one bite. That's ssam, a wrap.

Saying

"One cow, a hundred flavours"

Koreans have a saying, il du baek mi: "one cow, a hundred flavours," because every part of the animal is used. Korea has around 120 named retail cuts of beef, far more than most Western countries.
