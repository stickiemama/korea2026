The cards

Jeju dialect

"Field of wind"

In Jeju dialect, borom means wind (standard Korean baram) and wat means field (standard bat). Jeju's dialect is so different from standard Korean that mainland Koreans often can't understand older speakers, and UNESCO lists it as a critically endangered language.

Spot: other Jeju words on signs, like olle.
Crops

Buckwheat island

Jeju grows more buckwheat than anywhere else in South Korea. The volcanic soil drains fast and suits it, and it grows quickly. In autumn the fields are covered in tiny white flowers, and the seeds become noodles and the bingtteok crepes on our food page.

Flowers

A different field every season

Borom Wat plants new flowers each season: tulips and canola in spring, lavender and hydrangeas in summer, and buckwheat, pink muhly grass and cosmos in autumn. In late October the pink muhly grass is fading, but the white buckwheat is in bloom.

Fun

Tin cans and macadamias

Entry usually includes a ride on a little tin-can train that loops around the fields in about seven minutes, and a macadamia nut you crack open yourself. Macadamias have one of the hardest shells of any nut.

Sweet

Watching chocolate being made

The shop lets visitors watch chocolate being made and try samples. Chocolate comes from cacao beans grown in hot countries near the equator, so it's made here from imported beans, often flavoured with Jeju ingredients like hallabong or green tea.
