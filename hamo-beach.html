Crabs in every crack

We finished Fri 30 Oct at Hamo Beach at dusk, our best beach find of the week. We spotted plenty of crabs tucked into the nooks and crannies of the rock shelves, and it inspired a Hawaiian seafood dinner afterwards.

The cards

About the beach

Rock shelves

Why so many crabs?

Flat shelves of lava rock, cracked into countless gaps, give crabs perfect hiding places from birds and waves. At low tide, water stays trapped in pools between the shelves, so crabs and small creatures can stay wet while they wait for the sea to return.

Crab facts

Crab superpowers

Crabs breathe through gills, which they keep damp to breathe out of water. Many can grow back a lost leg or claw over several moults. And they walk sideways because their leg joints bend best that way.

Nearby

Moseulpo and the islands

Hamo sits beside Moseulpo port, where ferries leave for Gapado and Marado, Korea's southernmost island. Jeju Olle route 10 runs along this coast towards Songaksan.

Name

Also called Moseulpo Beach

Hamo Beach is sometimes called Moseulpo Beach. Swimming is no longer allowed here, but the shore is free to explore, ideal for rock pools rather than a dip.
