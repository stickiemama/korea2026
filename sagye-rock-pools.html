The plan

Why Sagye

Our pick

Best for crabs and pools

Sagye Coast is right next to Yongmeori and Sanbangsan, so it fits tomorrow perfectly. Around low tide the sea pulls back to show rock shelves, sandy patches and pools, with views of Sanbangsan behind and the little Hyeongjeseom ("Brother Islands") offshore.

Check the tide: search "사계항 물때" (Sagye Port tide) on Naver, or use the Badatime app, and aim for an hour either side of low tide.
Backup

If the tide is high

Hwasun Geummorae Beach, on the other side of Sanbangsan, has golden sand to dig in and rocks at its edges, a good backup for crab spotting if the pools at Sagye are underwater.

Ancient

Footprints from the past

Near Sagye, scientists found fossil footprints of ancient people and animals pressed into old volcanic sediment, thousands of years old. The site is protected, so it's look-don't-walk, but it's amazing to think people walked this same shore so long ago.

Field guide

Who you might meet

Crabs

Shore crabs

Small crabs hide under rocks and in cracks. The Asian shore crab often has red or purple spots and bands, like the one at Gwangchigi. Crabs walk sideways because their legs bend best that way.

Weird but true

Sea slaters

Those fast, many-legged creatures scuttling over the dry rocks are sea slaters, related to woodlice, not insects. Koreans nickname them "sea cockroaches," but they're harmless clean-up crew.

Pools

Anemones and snails

Sea anemones look like blobs of red jelly when the tide is out, then open their tentacles underwater to catch food. Turban snails and periwinkles graze on the algae. Tiny fish like gobies dart between rocks.

Rock pool rules

Be a good explorer

Wet your hands before touching creatures, lift rocks gently and put them back the same way up, return every creature where you found it, and leave the shellfish: the local haenyeo look after them. Keep off slippery green rocks, and always keep an eye on the sea.

Mission

Rock pool bingo

  1. Find a crab, an anemone, a snail and a fish. Bingo!
  2. Spot a sea slater racing over the rocks.
  3. Photograph Sanbangsan from the rock pools.
  4. Journal: the strangest creature of the day, and where you put it back.
