Part 1

The cards

Science

Why the water glows turquoise

Hamdeok's sand is pale and partly made of crushed shells. Sunlight passes through the shallow water, bounces off the light sand and comes back up as bright turquoise and emerald. Over deeper water or dark sand, the sea looks navy instead.

Compare: the colour of the sea here versus a black-rock beach.
Family beach

Shallow for a long way

The seabed slopes very gently, so you can wade a long way out and still be only waist-deep. That's why it's one of Jeju's most popular family swimming beaches in summer.

Volcanic

White sand, black rock

Black basalt rocks from Jeju's old lava flows frame both ends of the beach. The white-sand, black-rock, turquoise-water combination is the classic Jeju beach look.

Look for: rock pools at low tide, with tiny crabs and shellfish.
Oreum

Seoubong, the hill at the end

The grassy hill at the eastern end is Seoubong, one of Jeju's roughly 360 oreums (small volcanic cones). A short trail of about 15 minutes leads to views along the coast. It's yellow with canola flowers in spring and covered in silver grass in autumn.

Walking

On the Olle trail

Jeju Olle Route 19, one of the island's long-distance coastal walking routes, passes along this coast. Olle routes are named after the winding stone lanes that once led to Jeju homes, just like the one on our stone house page.

Rainy-day tip

Beach cafés

Hamdeok is lined with sea-view cafés, some with huge windows facing the water. On a grey day they're the best way to watch the waves stay warm and dry.

Our visit

Hamdeok in a typhoon

We visited on Sun 25 Oct while Typhoon Champi passed south of Jeju, bringing heavy rain, wind and big waves. Not the turquoise postcard, but a much wilder sea than most visitors get to see.

Mission
