The cards

Before you go

Rock history

Layers like a cake

Yongmeori is one of the oldest volcanic landforms on Jeju, over a million years old. Wave after wave of ash from eruptions in the sea settled into layers, and the ocean has since carved them into curves, ledges and caves. Each stripe is a page of the eruption's story.

Look for: how the layers tilt and curve. Which way was the volcano?
Name

The dragon's head

Yongmeori means "dragon's head." From above, the headland looks like a dragon diving into the sea. One legend says a Chinese emperor sent a geomancer to cut the dragon's back, to stop a great ruler from ever being born on Jeju.

Safety

Only open at the right tide

The path runs along the bottom of the cliffs, right at the waterline, so it closes at high tide and in strong wind or big waves. Check at the ticket booth, or tide times, before you go.

Legend

The mountain that was thrown

Remember the Hallasan legend? A hunter poked the sky god with his bow, the god tore off Hallasan's peak and threw it here, and it became Sanbangsan. Scientists say it's a lava dome: thick, sticky lava that piled up instead of flowing away, which is why it's shaped like a bell.

Temple

A temple in a cave

Sanbanggulsa is a Buddhist temple in a cave high up on Sanbangsan's side. Water drips from the cave ceiling into a small pool, and visitors sip it. One legend says the drops are the tears of a goddess who lives in the mountain.

Bring: legs for the steps up, and a camera for the view over the coast.
History

Shipwrecked for 13 years

In 1653 a Dutch trading ship wrecked on this coast. The survivors weren't allowed to leave Korea, and one of them, Hendrick Hamel, escaped 13 years later and wrote the first detailed European book about Korea. Look for the Hamel memorial nearby.

Mission

Coast detectives

  1. Count the layers in one section of the Yongmeori cliff.
  2. Find a cave or arch carved by the waves.
  3. Climb to Sanbanggulsa and taste (or touch) the dripping water.
  4. Find something about Hendrick Hamel.
