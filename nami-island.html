The cards

About Nami

Name

The young general

The island is named after General Nam I, a brilliant young Joseon general who was falsely accused of treason and executed in 1468 at just 28. A pile of stones on the island was said to mark his grave, and people believed taking a stone from it would bring bad luck.

Geography

An island made by a dam

Nami wasn't always an island. When the Cheongpyeong Dam was built downstream in 1944, the river rose and flooded the low land around it, leaving this half-moon of high ground surrounded by water.

Weird but true

The Republic of Naminara

Nami Island jokingly calls itself an independent country, the Naminara Republic, with its own flag, "passports" and even souvenir money. It's a playful idea: a country of imagination and fairy tales.

K-drama

The Winter Sonata effect

In 2002 the drama Winter Sonata filmed its romantic scenes along Nami's tree-lined paths. It became a huge hit in Japan and across Asia, and fans poured in, helping to start the Korean Wave of K-dramas abroad.

Science

Ginkgo, the living fossil

Ginkgo trees have barely changed in about 200 million years, since the age of the dinosaurs. They're so tough that a few survived the atomic bomb in Hiroshima. Female trees drop smelly seeds; roasted, the nut inside, eunhaeng, is a popular Korean snack.

Smell test: notice a cheesy pong on the path? That’s ginkgo seeds.
Wildlife

Free-roaming friends

Rabbits, squirrels and peacocks wander freely around the island. The squirrels here are mostly Korean red squirrels: grey-brown with tufted ears, busy burying nuts for winter.
