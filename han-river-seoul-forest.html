Picnic, bikes and deer

On Mon 2 Nov we had a very windy picnic at the riverside park in Jayang, rented bikes and rode along the Han River to Seoul Forest, where we saw the deer. We crossed the river twice and rode back: 3 hours in all.

The cards

About the river

The river

Seoul’s great river

The Han River flows about 500 km from the mountains in the east to the Yellow Sea, cutting Seoul into a northern half and a southern half. More than 25 bridges cross it inside the city, and almost the whole riverbank is lined with parks and bike paths.

History

The Miracle on the Han River

After the Korean War (1950–1953), South Korea was one of the poorest countries in the world. Over the next few decades it grew into a rich, high-tech country, so fast that people called it the "Miracle on the Han River."

Ride like a local

Ttareungyi

Seoul's green public bikes are called Ttareungyi, from ttareung, the "ding-ding" of a bike bell. You rent and return them with an app at docks all over the city, and the river paths are among the most popular routes.

Seoul Forest

From royal hunting ground to forest

In Joseon times, the Ttukseom area was a royal hunting and military parade ground. Later it held a water purification plant, a horse racetrack and a golf course. In 2005 it reopened as Seoul Forest, a big park planted by thousands of citizens.

Wildlife

Deer in the city

The deer at Seoul Forest are mostly sika deer, recognisable by the white spots on their summer coats. They live in a fenced woodland area, and visitors can watch them from walkways and a bridge above.
