Part 1

What we've eaten

Day 1 dinner

Black pork BBQ

Jeju's native black pig, heukdwaeji, has black hair and firmer, more flavourful meat. It's grilled in thick slices over charcoal, often with the skin on. Locals dip it in meljeot, a salty anchovy sauce warmed in a little dish right on the grill.

Remember: these pigs once lived under the outhouse. See the stone house page.
Heritage

A protected pig

The pure native Jeju black pig is so rare that the original breed has been named a Natural Monument of Korea, alongside things like ancient trees and rare birds. Most restaurant black pork comes from black pigs bred from it.

Day 2 lunch

Galchi, the hairtail fish

The long silver fish you had at lunch is galchi, called hairtail in English because its body narrows to a thin, hair-like tip at the end. Jeju's version, eun-galchi ("silver hairtail"), is caught one by one on fishing lines, so its shiny silver skin stays perfect. It's served grilled (galchi-gui), braised in a spicy sauce with radish (galchi-jorim), or in a clear soup with pumpkin (galchi-guk).

Why they deboned it: galchi has rows of fine bones along both sides of the backbone, so staff often lift the whole spine out for you.
Weird but true

A fish that stands up

Hairtail often hover upright in the water, head up and tail down, like silver ribbons. They have sharp fang-like teeth. At night, fishing boats hang bright lamps to attract them, and from the shore you can see rows of lights out at sea.

Look for: bright boat lights on the horizon at night.
Part 2

Still to try in Jeju

Jeju had little flat land for rice, so people relied on buckwheat, millet, seafood and pork.

Noodles

Gogi-guksu

Thick noodles in a rich pork-bone broth, topped with slices of boiled pork. Originally a dish for celebrations, it's now an everyday Jeju favourite.

From the haenyeo

Jeonbok-juk

Abalone porridge, made with rice cooked slowly with abalone and its green innards, which give it a pale green colour. It's a classic Jeju breakfast near the coast.

Island soup

Momguk

Pork broth thickened with mojaban, a local seaweed. Families traditionally cooked it for village feasts, weddings and funerals, so everyone could share.

Snack

Bingtteok

A thin buckwheat crepe rolled around seasoned radish. Simple and a bit chewy. Look for it at Dongmun Market or Olle Market.

Sweet

Hallabong everything

Hallabong, the citrus with a bump on top shaped like Hallasan, turns up as juice, chocolate, ice cream, jam and even beer. Tangerine season is just starting.
