Part 1

Gwangchigi

Volcanic

A beach made of lava

Gwangchigi is a wide stretch of black volcanic rock and dark sand, the remains of lava flows meeting the sea. At high tide much of it is underwater. At low tide a whole landscape of rock, channels and pools appears.

Check: tide times before you go. Low tide is when it’s at its best.
Famous photo

The green moss rocks

At low tide, many of the rocks are covered in bright green sea moss, with Seongsan Ilchulbong rising behind. It's one of the most famous photos on Jeju. The moss is greenest in spring and early summer, but the view of the peak is great all year.

Sunrise

One of the first places to see the sun

Gwangchigi is near Jeju's eastern edge, so it's one of the island's best sunrise spots, watching the sun come up beside the peak. It's also on the Jeju Olle walking trail.

Safety

After a typhoon

With a spring tide and leftover swell from Typhoon Champi, waves can suddenly wash over the low rocks. Stay well back from the water's edge, wear shoes with grip, and keep off rocks that are wet and dark.

Part 2

Sinyang Beach

Calm bay

A curved, sheltered bay

Sinyang Beach curves around a bay just south of Seopjikoji, and the headland shelters it from much of the wind and swell. That makes it one of the calmer beaches in this area, and a good choice the day after a storm.

Sport

Windsurfing beach

The shallow, sheltered water makes Sinyang a popular spot for windsurfing and kitesurfing. On a breezy day, look for colourful sails out on the bay.

Combine

Seopjikoji next door

The grassy headland of Seopjikoji, with its lighthouse and cliffs, is a short walk away. If the weather is good, walk the clifftop loop and look back at Seongsan Ilchulbong across the water.

Mission

Beach detectives

  1. Find out whether it’s high or low tide when we arrive.
  2. Take the classic photo: rocks in front, Seongsan Ilchulbong behind.
  3. Find a rock pool and look for a crab, a snail or a tiny fish.
  4. Journal: compare Gwangchigi with Hamdeok. Black or white sand?
