The cards

Pan stories

Name

Chicken "ribs" with no ribs

Dak means chicken and galbi means ribs, but dakgalbi is made from boneless chicken, usually thigh. It's a joke on the name of expensive beef and pork galbi.

History

The commoner’s galbi

Dakgalbi began in Chuncheon taverns in the 1960s as a cheap alternative to beef and pork galbi, popular with students and soldiers. It was so cheap and filling that people called it "commoner's galbi." Chuncheon now has whole streets of dakgalbi restaurants.

Map it: Nami Island is officially part of Chuncheon.
In the pan

What goes in

Chicken marinated in gochujang (red chilli paste), garlic and soy sauce, stir-fried with cabbage, sweet potato, rice cakes (tteok), onion and perilla leaves. Staff often cook it at your table, because it's easy to burn.

Best part?

Save room for fried rice

When most of the chicken is gone, ask for bokkeumbap: rice, seaweed and sauce fried in the leftover juices and pressed flat so it crisps on the bottom. Many Koreans say this is the best part.

Local partner

Chuncheon makguksu

Chuncheon's other famous dish is makguksu, cold buckwheat noodles. Spicy hot dakgalbi followed by cool noodles is the classic Chuncheon meal.
