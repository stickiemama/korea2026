Part 1

How a lava tube forms

Step 1A river of lava

Thousands of years ago, the Geomunoreum volcano in north-east Jeju erupted and sent rivers of runny lava flowing towards the sea.

Step 2A crust on top

The surface touched the cool air, hardened into rock and formed a roof. Underneath, insulated like a thermos, hot lava kept flowing.

Step 3It drains out

When the eruption stopped, the lava drained away downhill and left an empty tunnel behind. That's where you walked.

Part 2

The cards

Size

One of the longest in the world

The main tunnel is about 7.4 km long, and some surveys count nearly 9 km with its side passages. Only about 1 km is open to visitors. The world's longest lava tube is Kazumura Cave in Hawaii, at over 65 km.

Age

Older than you can imagine

Scientists' estimates range from about 70,000 to 300,000 years old, long before people lived on Jeju. Either way, this tunnel was here during the last Ice Age.

Look closer

The bathtub rings

The horizontal ridges and shelves along the walls are flow lines. Each one marks a level where the lava river ran for a while before dropping, like the ring a bath leaves when you drain it.

Look closer

Lava that dripped and froze

The small spikes on the ceiling are lava stalactites: drops of molten rock that dripped from the roof and hardened before they could fall. On the floor, wavy, ropy patterns show which way the last lava was flowing when it froze.

Photo spot

Turtle Rock

About 600 m in, a flat block of lava sits on the floor looking like a giant turtle. People say that from above, it's shaped like Jeju Island. It was probably a chunk of hardened crust that floated along on the lava river like a raft and got stuck.

The big finale

The 7.6 m lava column

The cave has an upper level above the main tunnel. Lava poured down through a hole between them and piled up like melted candle wax, building a column about 7.6 m tall, as high as a two-storey house. It's one of the largest lava columns known in the world.

Hidden residents

30,000 bats

About 30,000 bent-wing bats live in the closed parts of the cave, the largest bat colony known in Korea. Keeping people out of those sections protects them.

Discovery

Explored by a teacher and his students

In 1946, a local schoolteacher named Bu Jong-hyu led his students into the tunnel with flaming torches, with no lights, handrails or walkways. They mapped it into the darkness and brought it to the world's attention.

Name

"Very deep cave"

Manjang means "very deep" or "ten thousand jang long" (a jang is an old Korean unit of length), and gul means cave. People named it before anyone had measured how long it really was.

Famous

Part of a UNESCO trio

Manjanggul belongs to the Geomunoreum lava tube system. In 2007 UNESCO listed it as World Natural Heritage together with Hallasan and Seongsan Ilchulbong. It stays about 11–21°C all year, so it feels cool in summer and mild in winter.
