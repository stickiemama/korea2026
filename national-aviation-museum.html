The cards

About the museum

Pioneers

Korea’s first pilots

In 1922, Ahn Chang-nam became the first Korean to fly over Seoul, while Korea was under Japanese rule. Crowds of around 50,000 came to watch, and he became a hero.

Kwon Ki-ok became Korea's first woman pilot in the 1920s. She trained in China and flew for the independence movement, hoping one day to bomb the Japanese governor's office.

History

A flight school in California

In 1920 Korea's government-in-exile opened a flight school in Willows, California, funded by Korean farmers living in America. Its students trained on biplanes like the Standard J-1 to become pilots for a free Korea.

Look for: the Standard J-1 biplane in the museum.
Korean War

The first jet fighters

The F-86 Sabre hanging in the museum was one of the jets that fought in the Korean War (1950–1953), in the world's first big jet-versus-jet air battles, mostly against Soviet-built MiG-15s over the north. That links straight back to this morning at the DMZ.

Made in Korea

The T-50 Golden Eagle

The T-50 is Korea's own supersonic jet trainer, built by Korea Aerospace Industries and first flown in 2002. It can fly at about one and a half times the speed of sound. The Black Eagles, Korea's air force display team, fly a version of it, painted black and gold.

Fun fact: Korea now sells jets from the T-50 family to air forces in Indonesia, the Philippines, Thailand and Poland.
Big jet

A jumbo jet, sliced open

The museum has a cross-section of a real Boeing 747, so you can see the layers inside: passenger deck, cargo hold, and the cables and pipes hidden in between.

Try it

Jobs at the airport

The experience zones let visitors try jobs in aviation: flying a Black Eagles simulator, directing planes from a mock control tower, or training as cabin crew, including the evacuation slide. Most need booking and cost extra; entry to the museum is free.
