Straight after Ttarabi on Tue 27 Oct, we walked Bulgeun Oreum. A strong wind warning came through for Seogwipo's mid-mountain area that afternoon, so the forest's shelter was welcome.

Part 2

The cards

Name

The red oreum

Bulgeun means "red." The oreum is named after its reddish volcanic gravel, called scoria, or songi in Jeju. Scoria is lava that cooled full of gas bubbles, so it's light and crunchy, and turns red as the iron in it rusts.

Try: pick up a piece of red gravel. It’s surprisingly light for a rock.
Trees

A planted forest

Many of the tall, straight trees are Japanese cedar. In the 1970s Jeju planted huge numbers of them as windbreaks and to cover bare hillsides. Today they make dark, quiet, sweet-smelling forests.

Wildlife

Roe deer country

Roe deer (noru) live in the forests around the oreum, along with pheasants. Roe deer are small and shy, with big ears and a white patch on their bottom that flashes when they run.

History

Walls for royal horses

Jeju's hillsides are crossed by long, low stone walls called jatseong. Built centuries ago at different heights on Hallasan's slopes, they kept the royal horse herds in their pastures and out of the farmland below.

Look for: mossy stone walls running through the forest.
Compare

Grass oreum vs forest oreum

Some oreums, like Ttarabi, are covered in grass because they were grazed or cut for centuries. Others, like Bulgeun, grew over with trees. Same volcanic shape, very different feel.

Mission
